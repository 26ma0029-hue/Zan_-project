01 — About

Reading history
through ruins.

A Digital Humanities archive connecting architecture, geography, industrial history, images, sources and community memory.

The plateau regions and dense forests of Jharkhand hold a quiet record of neglected historical and architectural heritage. Ruined forts and palaces of medieval Chero and Nagvanshi dynasties sit alongside disused colonial mining structures, railway outposts, and forest bungalows. These decaying structures serve as physical artifacts of tribal history, early industrial extraction, and shifting regional power dynamics that remain absent from mainstream archives.

A Digital Humanities approach brings these forgotten landscapes to light by systematically recording their physical state, geographic coordinates, and surrounding oral traditions. Documenting these spaces through structured metadata, field photography, and geospatial mapping creates a permanent, accessible digital record. This archival effort preserves vulnerable cultural narratives, protects local memory from physical decay, and offers researchers a comprehensive framework to study the region's historical geography.

“A ruin is not simply a building that has disappeared from use; it is an archive that continues to speak through place, material and memory.”

Jharia Coalfield

Jharia Coalfield in Jharkhand, India, contains underground fires burning continuously since 1916 due to unscientific colonial mining and spontaneous combustion. The Jharia Coalfield was largely abandoned and affected by mine-fire and subsidence conditions.

03 — Metadata Framework

Every place
needs a record.

A reusable metadata structure makes photographs, geography, historical description and source provenance easier to study.

Definition: Metadata is structured information about an archive item. In this project, a record connects a place name and location with its image, description, contributor and reference source.

Digital Humanities value: The same framework can later be expanded with GPS coordinates, architectural condition, field photographs, oral-history transcripts, dates, tags, map layers and preservation status.
